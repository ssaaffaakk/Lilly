# Pass-7c refused: it reads photographs worse than what is shipped

The pass-7c run (kernel `lilly-ocr`, 31 Aug) finished COMPLETE, its crop-level
install gate passed, and it therefore packaged the trained weights as the reader
the app loads. It reported no numbers at all. `train_ocr.py` writes its
before/after rates to its own stdout, and a child process's stdout never reaches
the Kaggle log, so 21 minutes of training logged exactly one line — the command
— and nothing else. `python -u` had been added for precisely this and changed
nothing, because buffering was never the cause.

The weights were measured here instead, on the same 40 photographs and the same
answer key as the published figures:

| | pooled words | per photograph | diacritic | invented words |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| shipped pass-6 (`RESULTS-ocr-realcrops.md`) | **45.0%** | **54.7%** | 44.0% | **180** |
| pass-7c candidate | 41.0% | 53.6% | 44.0% | 208 |

Worse on both word rates, no better on diacritics, and 28 more words invented
out of brickwork and foliage. The candidate was not installed. The shipped
reader is back in `models/lilly/read/lilly.pth`, kept alongside it as
`models/lilly/keep-read-pass6/lilly.pth`, and the trained weights stay in
`models/kaggle-output/lilly-read-trained.zip` for inspection only.

The crop gate passing while the photograph rates fell is the finding worth
keeping: the gate measures crops, the published claim is per photograph, and
nothing in the run ever read a photograph. `Lilly_OCR_Kaggle.ipynb` now tees
child output into Output, and gates on the photographs before packaging.

Everything below describes the **refused candidate**, not the shipped reader.

# What the reader reads off a real photograph

28 photographs from Wikimedia Commons, none of them ours, transcribed by eye before the reader was run on them. 12 more carried no legible text and are excluded from the rates below — a photograph with nothing to read cannot be read wrongly.

| | words found | of | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| All words | 153 | 373 | **41.0%** |
| Words with č ć đ š ž | 11 | 25 | **44.0%** |
| The same words, diacritics folded away | 15 | 25 | **60.0%** |

Weighting every photograph equally instead of every word, the reader finds **53.6%** of the words on a photograph. The two differ because the text is not spread evenly: `Spanish_square_08034.JPG` alone holds 144 of the 373 words in the answer key. The per-photograph figure is the one that describes pointing a camera at a sign; the pooled figure describes reading a wall of text.

Two independent readers transcribed these photographs without seeing each other's work or the machine's, and agreed on 91.2% of the words either of them saw. Only the words both saw are in the answer key, so that agreement is also the ceiling on how precise anything here can be.

The reader also returned 208 words that are not on any sign in these photographs. That is the cost a user pays for text the detector invented out of brickwork and foliage, and it is not visible in a recall figure.
